# Storage, freezing, thawing and reheating

- **Source:** Joe's Meal Pack, Oct 20 to Nov 4, 2026, built by Thrifty and kept by Meal Plan. Copied from the pack.

| Food | Fridge | Freezer | Reheat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pulled pork | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months, flat bags with juice | Thaw overnight. Microwave covered with a splash of juice, 50% power, stir every minute. Or skillet with a splash of water. |
| Shredded chicken | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months | Same as pork; add a splash of broth (Better Than Bouillon + water) so it stays moist. |
| Cooked pork chops | 3 to 4 days | Best within 1 month, each in its own quart Ziploc freezer bag | See R18. Low power, slow. |
| Tortellini alfredo | 3 days | Not recommended (sauce separates) | Microwave at 50% power, 1 min at a time, stirring, with a splash of milk. Or skillet over low with milk. |
| Cheesy garlic bread | 3 days, in a quart Ziploc freezer bag | 1 month, in a quart Ziploc freezer bag | Oven 350°F 8 min, then 2 to 3 min more to re-crisp (uncovered, it may brown or dry faster). Microwave makes it chewy. |
| Baked potatoes | 3 to 4 days (cool, then fridge) | Not great | Microwave 1.5 to 3 min, cut open halfway. Or oven 375°F 15 to 20 min for crisp skin. |
| Cooked wings (deli or homemade) | 3 to 4 days | 2 months | Air Fry mode 375°F, 8 to 12 min, flip once. |
| Cooked chicken breast | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months | Slice, microwave 50% power with a splash of broth, covered. |
| Hot deli food from the store | Eat within 2 hours of pickup or refrigerate. Use leftovers within 3 to 4 days. | | |
| Opened lunchmeat | 3 to 5 days | Unopened packs freeze 1 to 2 months | Thaw in the fridge. |
| Raw ground beef | 1 to 2 days | 3 to 4 months | Thaw in fridge the night before. |
| Raw chicken breasts (fresh) | 1 to 2 days (or by use-by date) | 9 months | Thaw in fridge 1 day. |

General rules: get cooked food into the fridge within 2 hours. Freeze flat in labeled bags (what + date). Thaw in the fridge, not on the counter. When in doubt, throw it out.

## Thawing
- Thick pork chops need about 24 hours in the fridge. 4 lb of GV wing sections needs about 24 to 36 hours.
- Forgot to thaw a bag? Set the sealed bag in a bowl of cold water for 30 to 60 minutes, or microwave on defrost in short bursts.

## Doneness preference
Joe likes pork chops medium rare: pull at about 135°F, then rest. (USDA guidance is 145°F with a 3 minute rest.) Chops headed for the freezer get pulled at about 130°F, since reheating adds a few degrees (R17). Joe's words to Thrifty (Oct 20, 2026): "the pork chops are an excelent thing to cook to med rare ahead of time and eat one for dinner then (sunday) then microwave the rest for dinners form frozen over the week"

## Work bowl microwave reheat

**From Oven Air Fry Chef, not yet tested by Joe.** Updated Oct 25, 2026 for the mashed potato and shredded chicken bowls (replaces the old chicken, roasted potato, corn, and broccoli version).

Mashed potato and shredded chicken bowl (with torn habanero jack and candied jalapenos), dumped from the quart Ziploc onto a sturdy paper plate, or two stacked. Times are for a 1000 W microwave at level 5 (50%). The work microwave's wattage is unknown: if its label says 700-900 W, use 70% for the same times; if it says 1100-1200 W, use 40%.

Fridge-cold (Mon, Tue):
1. Spread the bowl in a ring about 1 inch thick with an empty center.
2. Sprinkle 2 Tbsp water, broth, or whole milk over it and cover with a damp paper towel.
3. Microwave at 50% for 4 min. Stir outside to center and re-form the ring.
4. Microwave at 50% for 4 more min.
5. Stir, re-cover, and let it stand 3 min.
6. Done at 165F in 2-3 spots. If short, 1 more min at 50%.

Partly frozen (Wed, Thu):
1. Break up the icy clumps through the bag first.
2. Same plate, ring, 2 Tbsp liquid, and damp paper towel.
3. Microwave at 50% for 5 min. Stir, break up the ice, and re-form the ring.
4. Microwave at 50% for 5 more min. If any ice is left, 1-min steps at 50%, stirring each time.
5. Let it stand 3 min. Done at 165F in 2-3 spots. If short, 1 more min.

The cheese folds in during the stirs.
